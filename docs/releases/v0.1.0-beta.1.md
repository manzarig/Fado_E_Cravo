**The radio is finished for v1.** It has its own walnut cabinet now: the needle slides along the dial as you tune, the valves glow through the vents, the speaker cloth trembles with the music, and the radio warms up before it plays. This beta is the release candidate for v1. Only the music is still a placeholder.

### What's new since alpha.2
- **The animated radio.** A new model replaces the placeholder block, made with GeckoLib:
  - the needle follows the frequency, live while you turn the dial;
  - the tuning knob turns with the needle, and the power knob clicks round when you switch the radio on or off;
  - the dial, the pilot lamp and the valves behind the back and top vents glow while the radio is on;
  - the speaker cloth moves with the programme, harder the clearer the station comes in;
  - music notes rise from a radio that is playing.
- **Valve warm-up.** A radio you switch on stays quiet for 2.5 seconds while the valves warm up and the glow comes up, then it joins the programme where it is.
- **A faint light.** A radio that is on gives light level 4, so the lamp shows at night.
- **Songs finish.** A song still playing when the time of day changes now plays to its end before the next part of the schedule starts. Dawn and dusk no longer cut songs off half-way.
- **Emissora Lusitana closes down at night.** After its sign-off at eight it plays one interval chime and goes quiet until five in the morning, as the sign-off says. Before, it repeated the chime all night.
- **A textured tuning screen:** a walnut board, brass rivets, a paper dial and a glass readout.
- **Clearer Portuguese.** The power button now names what it does ("Ligar" / "Desligar") next to the state lamp ("Ligado" / "Desligado"), and the hints read better.
- **Your own recordings.** The project now includes the Estúdio, a small local app that puts your own recordings on the radio. It runs from the source checkout and is not part of the jar. See "Adding your own recordings" in the README.

### What's inside
- **The AM radio.** Craft it from planks, a note block, copper and redstone dust. Sneak + right-click switches it on or off, and a redstone pulse toggles it too.
- **Tuning.** Right-click for the dial, which covers 530–1600 kHz. Scroll for 1 kHz steps, hold Shift for 10 kHz, use the left and right arrow keys, or click and drag the dial. A whistle sounds as you reach a station.
- **Three stations.** Emissora Lusitana (670 kHz), Rádio Tejo (1035 kHz) and Rádio Atlântico (1440 kHz, night only, weak signal). Each one runs its own schedule across dawn, day, dusk and night.
- **Range and volume.** About 64 blocks, with the sound fading as you walk away. The volume follows the "Jukebox/Note Blocks" slider.
- **Operator command.** `/fadoecravo breaking <station>` puts a breaking-news bulletin on a station, and `/fadoecravo breaking clear` takes it off the air.

### Requirements
- Minecraft Java Edition **1.21.1**
- Fabric Loader **0.20.1** or newer
- [Fabric API](https://modrinth.com/mod/fabric-api) (tested with 0.116.17+1.21.1)
- [GeckoLib](https://modrinth.com/mod/geckolib) **4.9** or newer (tested with 4.9.3). It is now used for the radio model, so it is really required.
- Optional: [Mod Menu](https://modrinth.com/mod/modmenu) to see the mod in the in-game mod list

### Known limitations
- **Placeholder music:** the pieces are synthesized test music until real recordings arrive (planned for v1.1). You can already swap in your own with the Estúdio.
- With `doDaylightCycle false`, radios switched on at different times **aren't in sync**.
- The warm-up only plays for a switch that you see happen. A radio that was already on when you arrive simply plays.
